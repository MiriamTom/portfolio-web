Full Research Abstract

AI Assistants, Impostor Syndrome & Future Anxiety

Authors: Miriam Tomášová | Dept. of Informatics, ČVUT

This paper examines the relationship between AI assistant usage frequency, impostor syndrome, and future anxiety. The goal is to identify whether AI tools lead to synergistic effects (increased productivity and confidence) or psychological conflicts (doubts about abilities, increased anxiety). The research focuses on technical students, analyzing correlations between AI usage frequency and selected psychological variables.

Methodology: Quantitative survey (N=215 students), standardized scales: Clance Impostor Phenomenon Scale, Future Anxiety Scale, custom AI usage frequency scale. Statistical analysis includes Pearson correlation and linear regression.

Preliminary Findings: Mild positive correlation between intensive AI use (>5× daily) and higher impostor scores, with a significant moderating effect — students who perceive AI as a "learning tool" show lower anxiety. Passive reliance on AI increases impostor feelings.

Conclusions: Opens questions about designing AI tools that strengthen autonomy and confidence. Results to be presented at end of 2026.
